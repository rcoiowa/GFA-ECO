# EJWRH Medication & MOUD Policy

Ernest & Johnnie White Recovery House · 1414 12th Street, Des Moines, Iowa 50314

## The commitment first

**Medication for opioid or alcohol use disorder (MOUD/MAT) — buprenorphine, methadone,
naltrexone, and others prescribed to you — is recovery, not a compromise of it.** Taking
your prescribed medication is never a violation, never grounds for denial or discharge, and
never treated differently from any other prescription. Nobody at EJWRH will pressure you to
change, taper, or stop a medication — those decisions belong to you and your prescriber.

## Your medications stay yours

- **You keep your own medications and take them yourself.** EJWRH and Grace For Addictions
  do not routinely administer, dispense, or dose anyone's medication.
- There is **no house lockbox requirement and no house medication safe requirement.** Your
  medications remain securely in your own possession — stored so housemates can't get to
  them, which protects you and them.
- Where a specific situation genuinely warrants it, an **individual secure-storage
  arrangement can be set up case by case** with the House Manager — an exception built with
  you, never a blanket rule.

## What the house needs to know

- At intake, staff review your current medications with you and record the minimum: the
  medication name, how it's stored, and whether a prescriber is on file. **No dosages, no
  diagnoses, and no medical history are collected.** If you take no medications, staff
  simply record that the review happened.
- **New prescriptions or material changes: tell the House Manager** so the residence's
  safety and storage documentation stays current — that's the whole ask.
- For controlled medications, all that's needed is **reasonable evidence they are lawfully
  prescribed** — current labeled packaging or a medication list is preferred; targeted
  verification only where genuinely needed. Nobody asks for your clinical records.

## If something goes missing

Report lost or missing medication to staff immediately. Missing controlled medication is a
safety issue for the whole house and is documented and handled without assuming fault.
