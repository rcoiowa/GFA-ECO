# EJWRH Return-to-Use Response Policy

Ernest & Johnnie White Recovery House · 1414 12th Street, Des Moines, Iowa 50314

Return to use is part of many recovery stories. At EJWRH it is met with care first —
and never with an automatic consequence.

## The sequence — every time

1. **Care conversation.** Before anything else, a staff member sits down with you: how are
   you, what happened, are you safe right now.
2. **Safety assessment.** Immediate risks first — yours and the house's. If medical care is
   needed, it comes before everything else (911 for emergencies; 988 for crisis support).
3. **Support response.** Where it fits: intensified recovery support, more frequent coaching
   contact, a clinical or higher-level-of-care referral — built with you, not done to you.
4. **Community fit.** An honest look at the safety of the house community and whether the
   program can keep supporting you well right now.
5. **Documented human decision.** A person decides, explains the decision to you, and
   documents it — the care-response decision is facilitated by the Operator, the designated
   Liaison, and/or authorized Executive Leadership. Possible outcomes: remain with added
   support, a modified support plan, temporary transition to a higher level of care, or — as
   a last resort — discharge.

**No screening result ends a residency by itself.** There is no automatic discharge in this
house, and the software the house runs on is built so none can exist.

## What stays true throughout

- Your prescribed medications, including MOUD, are never part of the problem — a
  prescription-consistent result is lawful medication, not return to use.
- A result you contest is recorded as contested/unconfirmed while it is verified (see the
  Screening Policy) — this sequence runs on confirmed reality, not on a disputed strip.
- Honesty helps you. Telling staff before a screen is always better received than a
  surprise result — the sequence above starts at step 1 either way.
- If residency does end, it ends with a plan: belongings handled respectfully, referrals
  offered, and the door to Grace For Addictions recovery support stays open.
- Iowa's Good Samaritan protections may apply when someone in good faith seeks emergency
  help for an overdose — **calling 911 for yourself or a housemate is always the right
  call, and this house will never punish it.**
