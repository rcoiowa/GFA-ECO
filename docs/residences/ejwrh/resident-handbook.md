# EJWRH Resident Handbook

Ernest & Johnnie White Recovery House · 1414 12th Street, Des Moines, Iowa 50314
Office: 515-220-8771 · ejwrh@rcoiowa.org

Welcome home. This handbook explains daily life at EJWRH. It is here to answer questions,
not to trip you up — if something in it is unclear, ask. Acknowledging this handbook means
you received it and know where to look; the only document you sign is the Participant &
Residency Agreement.

## 1. The three phases

Your time here moves through three phases. Moving up is a conversation and a decision made
with staff based on how you are doing — never an automatic day count.

**Phase 1 — Foundation.** Land, stabilize, get connected. You will build your recovery plan,
meet the house, and start work or education planning. Curfew is 9:00 PM.

**Phase 2 — Growth.** Consistency and responsibility. Working toward 30 hours a week of
work, school, training, or volunteering. Curfew is 10:00 PM Sunday–Thursday, 11:00 PM
Friday–Saturday.

**Phase 3 — Leadership & Transition.** Mentor newer residents, lead in the house, and build
your transition plan. Curfew is 11:00 PM Sunday–Thursday, midnight Friday–Saturday.

Curfew never goes past midnight, in any phase.

## 2. Recovery support — your pathway counts

Qualifying recovery-support activities each week: **4 in Phase 1, 3 in Phase 2, 2 in
Phase 3.** What counts is broad on purpose: mutual-aid meetings of any tradition (12-Step,
SMART, Refuge, faith-based, cultural), recovery coaching sessions, structured peer support.
**No specific pathway is required — ever.** A sponsor or mentor is strongly encouraged and
never mandatory. Activities you already do with your coach or navigator count; nobody logs
the same thing twice.

## 3. Curfew, passes, and overnights

- Be home by your phase curfew unless you have an approved pass.
- Passes (including overnight passes) are requested from the house manager in advance —
  approval considers your phase, safety, and plan.
- Work schedules that conflict with curfew are accommodated with staff approval — tell the
  house manager your schedule.

## 4. House life

- **House meetings:** attendance is expected; the schedule is posted in the house.
- **Chores:** everyone carries a share; assignments are posted and rotate.
- **Guests:** common areas only, during posted hours, sober; no overnight guests.
- **Quiet hours:** [PENDING — OPERATOR: posted quiet hours.]
- **Rooms:** keep your space clean; room and bed assignments can change with notice.
- **House communication:** house announcements currently run through the house GroupMe.
  Over time announcements move into RecoveryOS — and the commitment is that it will never
  be more work than GroupMe.
- **Neighbors:** we are guests of this neighborhood — noise, parking, and property lines
  are respected; being a good neighbor is part of recovery leadership.

## 5. Money

Program fees and payment schedule are in your Participant & Residency Agreement. One rule
worth repeating: if paying is going to be hard this week, say so **before** it is missed.
It is a conversation, and there is always a conversation to have.

## 6. Safety and emergencies

- **Emergency: call 911 first**, then notify staff.
- **Crisis support: call or text 988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), any time.
- **Iowa Warm Line (non-crisis peer support): 844-775-9276.**
- Naloxone (Narcan) location: [PENDING — OPERATOR: posted location in the house.]
- Fire evacuation routes and the severe-weather shelter location are posted in the house.
  Staff walk every new resident through them during intake week.
- If you are worried about another resident's immediate safety, tell staff right away —
  that is care, not snitching.

## 7. Alcohol, drugs, and medications

The house is alcohol- and illicit-drug-free. Screening happens per the Screening Policy you
acknowledged. Your prescribed medications — including MOUD — are yours, are never a
violation, and are stored per the Medication & MOUD Policy. If you return to use, what
happens next starts with a care conversation, not a discharge — the Return-to-Use Response
Policy explains the sequence.

## 8. Your recovery plan

Shortly after admission you and your coach build your Individual Recovery Plan — what
matters to you, your goals, your people, your next steps. It is generated from the real
work you are already doing; reviews update it, and you own it.

## 9. Moving out

Transition is a goal, not a threat. In Phase 3 your transition plan gets concrete: housing,
income, support network, and what stays in place after you move. When you leave — planned or
not — belongings are handled respectfully, and the door to recovery support does not close.

## 10. Problems and complaints

If something is wrong — with the house, with staff, with a decision — the Resident Rights &
Grievance Process document explains exactly how to raise it, and retaliation for raising it
is prohibited. Your first stop can always be any staff member you trust.
