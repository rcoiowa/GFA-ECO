## Structural Framework

Grace For Addictions serves as the **contracted recovery program and technical services
provider** to the Operator. GFA is not a co-owner, co-operator, landlord, property operator,
partner, or joint venturer of the Operator or of W & W Properties, and GFA is not the
"operator" of the recovery residence. The Operator retains sole responsibility and ultimate
authority for housing operations, resident agreements, resident program fees, financial
management, property obligations, and housing-related legal responsibilities. This
separation supports NARR certification, Fair Housing compliance, nonprofit integrity, and
clean liability separation between the entities; the characterization of resident
occupancy under Iowa landlord-tenant law (Iowa Code ch. 562A) is addressed in Article 11.

*Role continuity:* GFA's Executive Director manages day-to-day contracted program services
and works directly with the House Manager. Functional roles in this Agreement are defined by
function, not by the individual holding them; a change in the person holding a role does not
affect this Agreement.

## Article 1 — Relationship of the Parties

**1.1 Independent Contractor.** Service Provider is an independent contractor. Service
Provider is not an employee, partner, joint venturer, co-owner, or co-operator of Operator
or of W & W Properties, and nothing in this Agreement creates any agency except as expressly
stated. **Neither party may bind the other to any obligation unless separately authorized in
writing.**

**1.2 Operator Role.** Operator is the recovery house operator, leasing the Premises from
W & W Properties. Curtis White is the founding sponsor whose vision and property provision
make this program possible. Operator retains sole authority and responsibility for: housing
operations; resident agreements; resident program fees (establishment, reduction, waiver,
collection, and accounts); financial management; property lease obligations, maintenance,
and compliance; Operator-employed staffing (including the House Manager); housing-related
legal responsibilities; and ultimate housing and residency decision authority as set out in
Article 6.

**1.3 Service Provider Role.** GFA provides the contracted services in Article 2:
recovery-support services, recovery coaching, navigation, peer-support coordination,
program-development services, RecoveryOS/VRCC infrastructure, policy and document
development support, training and technical assistance, NARR-readiness and certification
support, program-quality support, referral and network development, recovery/reentry
support, and reporting/documentation support within its authorized scope. GFA's name may
appear on program materials it authors in its service-provider capacity. GFA does **not**
own resident fees, manage Operator financial accounts, hold housing authority, or operate
the property.

**1.4 No Personal Guarantee.** Each signatory signs solely in the representative capacity
stated in Article 14. Neither party, and no individual signatory, is personally financially
liable for the other party's obligations.

**1.5 Nonprofit Integrity.** All services are consistent with Grace For Addictions'
501(c)(3) charitable mission, are provided at fair market value for the defined services,
and constitute an arms-length professional services arrangement.

## Article 2 — Scope of Services

**PHASE 1 · PROGRAM BUILD-OUT & LAUNCH · Milestone Deliverables**

- **2.1.A Complete Operational System** — Men's Resident Handbook (trauma-informed,
  reentry-aware, NARR-aligned); Staff Operations Manual (intake, drug testing, incident
  response, documentation); participant forms and workflows (intake, Individual Recovery
  Plan, screening log, grievance); house rules, three-phase structure, curfew framework,
  return-to-use response.
- **2.1.B Resident Agreement & Safety-Response Documentation** — Participant & Residency
  Agreement (program participation, not a lease) and emergency safety-response
  documentation, each prepared for Operator approval and, where legal characterization is
  implicated, qualified legal review (Article 11).
- **2.1.C NARR Level II Readiness** — full NARR Standard 3.0 compliance crosswalk and
  self-assessment; site readiness checklist; certification application support.
- **2.1.D RecoveryOS / VRCC Digital Platform** — configuration per Article 7.
- **2.1.E Staff Training** — House Manager and peer mentor orientation; naloxone/Narcan
  training; screening protocol and documentation standards.

**PHASE 2 · ONGOING PROGRAM SUPPORT SERVICES · Monthly Retainer**

- **2.2.A Program Support & Compliance** — NARR Level II compliance monitoring and annual
  renewal support; Iowa HHS reporting compliance guidance; program-quality support.
- **2.2.B Recovery & Reentry Support** — recovery coaching via credentialed peer coaches;
  Individual Recovery Plan development and coordination; reentry-informed coordination
  (employment, benefits, legal navigation); crisis response and 24/7 on-call program
  coverage.
- **2.2.C Wrap-Around Services** — transportation coordination (recovery meetings, medical
  appointments, employment); alumni support and GFA recovery community network connection.
- **2.2.D Referral & Network Development** — program materials and referral distribution
  content; development of referral relationships (courts, probation, reentry programs,
  treatment providers); RecoveryOS intake portal management and intake screening support.
  **Formal referral agreements that bear on housing or residency are executed by the
  Operator**; GFA may execute agreements limited to its own contracted services.

## Article 3 — Operator's Responsibilities

Operator shall: maintain the Premises per the W & W Properties lease; furnish all ten
resident beds; establish, disclose, and collect resident program fees per Article 4 and
maintain the related financial records; pay all property-related expenses (rent, water,
electric, gas/heat, trash, internet, property insurance); carry premises liability insurance
naming Service Provider as additional insured; pay Service Provider invoices within ten (10)
business days of receipt; cooperate in good faith with the contracted services and not
obstruct Service Provider's performance of them, **while retaining the housing and residency
decision authority described in Article 6**; and introduce reentry referral relationships to
Service Provider for development under Article 2.2.D.

## Article 4 — Resident Program Fees (Operator Authority)

**4.1 Standard Resident Program Fees.** The standard resident program fees established by
the Operator are:

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Shared room | $175 per week, or $650 per month when paid in advance |
| Single private room | $200 per week, or $750 per month when paid in advance |
| One-time move-in fee | $250 (nonrefundable) |
| Live-Out participation (when applicable) | $25 per week |

These amounts constitute EJWRH's standard published rates and shall be used for general
program administration, resident disclosures, budgeting, and prospective admissions unless
the Operator approves a different amount as permitted below.

**4.2 Operator Authority to Reduce Fees.** Ernest & Johnnie White Recovery House, L.L.C.,
as Operator, retains sole authority over resident program fees and may approve a reduced
rate, waiver, credit, scholarship, payment accommodation, hardship arrangement, sponsorship
arrangement, or other individualized fee agreement when the Operator determines that doing
so is appropriate. Individualized reductions may include: (1) honoring a prior written or
otherwise verifiable agreement with an existing resident; (2) financial hardship;
(3) reentry or transitional circumstances; (4) employment or benefits timing;
(5) scholarship or charitable support; (6) third-party funding; or (7) another circumstance
approved by the Operator. Any reduced arrangement shall identify only the minimum
necessary: the approved amount, effective date, duration or review date, and approving
authority — no unnecessary financial histories or narratives. An individualized reduction
does not change the standard published rate, does not create an entitlement or precedent
for any other resident, and must be administered consistently and without unlawful
discrimination. An individualized agreement may reduce or accommodate the standard fee but
may not increase a resident above the applicable published rate without a separate written
resident agreement.

**4.3 Existing Prior Agreements.** A valid individualized fee arrangement existing before
this Agreement's execution may continue according to its terms unless the Operator and the
resident mutually agree to modify it.

**4.4 Payment, Hardship, and Late Charges.** Consistent with the resident-facing policy:
weekly payments may be made at any point during the week, and a payment still outstanding
may be addressed at the Sunday house meeting; monthly payments are due on or before the 1st
of the month; no daily late charge applies during a resident's first thirty (30) days of
residency; thereafter a delinquent payment may incur **$5 per day, up to a maximum of $50
per delinquent payment period**; the daily charge is suspended while the resident is
compliant with an approved hardship/payment arrangement and may be waived based on advance
communication or documented circumstances; and **late payment alone never automatically
terminates residency** — persistent nonpayment is addressed through the documented human
decision process in Article 6.

**4.5 Fee Ownership and GFA's Financial Role.** All resident program fees are established
by, collected by, and belong solely to Operator. Service Provider has no ownership of,
percentage interest in, or claim to resident program fees; does not handle, hold, or manage
Operator's fee accounts; and does not assume Operator's vacancy or nonpayment risk. GFA may
provide budgeting support, financial-planning assistance, benefits/resource navigation, and
help developing a resident payment arrangement — but Operator retains sole authority to
establish, reduce, waive, approve, and collect resident fees and to manage housing financial
risk.

## Article 5 — Service Provider Compensation

Compensation under this Article is professional-service compensation paid by Operator to
Service Provider for the defined services in Article 2. It is separate from, and creates no
interest in, resident program fees (Article 4.5).

**5.1 Phase 1 Milestone Fees** (one-time, earned on delivery; non-refundable once the
corresponding deliverable is substantially completed):

| Deliverable | Fee | When earned |
|---|---|---|
| Complete Operational System | $1,500 | On delivery |
| Resident Agreement + safety-response documentation | $500 | On delivery |
| NARR Level II readiness package | $1,000 | On delivery |
| RecoveryOS/VRCC platform setup | $750 | On go-live |
| Staff training | $750 | On completion |
| **Phase 1 total** | **$4,500** | |
| Annual NARR renewal support | $500/year | At renewal |

**5.2 Phase 1 Payment Schedule.** The Phase 1 total is payable in three equal installments
of $1,500. Installments are first invoiced in the month occupancy first reaches 70% (7 of 10
beds), then in each of the two following months. If occupancy falls below 70% between
installments, the next installment is deferred until 70% occupancy has been maintained for
thirty (30) consecutive days. The Phase 1 schedule does not delay the monthly retainer,
which begins per Section 5.3 in the first month of resident occupancy.

**5.3 Monthly Retainer — Service-Volume Schedule.** The monthly retainer compensates the
defined Phase 2 services at fair market value. Because the volume of coaching, navigation,
crisis coverage, and support services scales with the number of residents served, the
retainer is set by the service-volume schedule below, measured by the number of residents
actively participating in the program on the first day of the billing month (Operator
provides the count on the first business day of each month):

| Residents served | Monthly retainer |
|---|---|
| 6 or more | $1,200 |
| 5 | $1,000 |
| 4 | $800 |
| 3 | $600 |
| 2 | $400 |
| 1 | $200 |
| 0 | $0 |

The full $1,200 retainer resumes immediately upon return to six or more residents. **This
schedule is a fair-market-value fee for services actually scaled to residents served; it is
not a share of, or participation in, Operator revenue, and Service Provider acquires no
interest in resident fees through it.**

**5.4 Invoicing and Payment.** Service Provider invoices Operator on the first business day
of each month for the prior month's retainer. Operator pays within ten (10) business days.
If an invoice is thirty (30) or more days past due, Service Provider may suspend ongoing
services on written notice until payment is received.

**5.5 Annual Review.** The retainer schedule and this Article are reviewed annually at
renewal. Either party may propose adjustments on sixty (60) days' written notice; any change
requires a written amendment signed by both parties.

## Article 6 — Admission, Discharge & Residency Authority

**6.1 Operator Authority.** Operator retains the legally operative housing and residency
decision authority — including admission, residency terms, and discharge — unless a defined
decision is explicitly delegated to Service Provider in writing.

**6.2 Service Provider Support.** GFA provides, in support of Operator decisions: screening
support; recovery-readiness information; program recommendations; coaching and navigation
input; safety-support information; and documentation and RecoveryOS workflows.

**6.3 RecoveryOS Boundaries.** RecoveryOS may organize evidence, present readiness,
document decisions, create follow-ups, and support workflows. **RecoveryOS never
automatically admits or discharges a resident**; approval of an application and admission to
residency are separate deliberate human acts.

**6.4 Return to Use.** A positive or concerning screening result is met with the following
sequence, always: care conversation → immediate safety/circumstances assessment →
intensified support as appropriate → higher-level-of-care referral where appropriate →
community-safety/continued-fit evaluation → **documented human decision**. **No screening
result automatically terminates residency.**

## Article 7 — RecoveryOS / VRCC Platform

Service Provider provides and maintains the RecoveryOS / Virtual Recovery Community Center
platform as infrastructure supporting application and intake, admission readiness, document
delivery, consent, resident support, coaching and navigation, recovery planning, service
documentation, compliance evidence, reporting, and follow-up. The parties agree: software
does not replace human authority; application approval and admission are separate acts; the
software never automatically discharges anyone; residents may sign documents electronically
or on paper with no adverse consequence for choosing paper; consent and information
disclosure are governed by their own recorded authorizations, separate from documents;
access is role- and permission-controlled; sensitive information is minimized to what the
work genuinely requires; and the relationship between the resident and the people supporting
him takes priority over unnecessary documentation.

## Article 8 — Policy & Document Authority

Service Provider may develop, recommend, maintain, and technically implement program and
recovery-support policies, documents, and systems within its professional scope. **Operator
retains approval authority for any policy that materially establishes housing terms,
resident financial obligations, resident agreements, property rules, housing
admission/discharge authority, or Operator legal responsibilities.** Where a policy
materially affects both parties' responsibilities, it takes effect on joint ratification.
Technical implementation in RecoveryOS never constitutes policy approval.

## Article 9 — Insurance & Liability

Service Provider carries: commercial general liability ($1M/$2M), professional liability
($1M), and workers' compensation; Operator is named additional insured on the CGL. Operator
carries premises liability insurance ($1M minimum); Service Provider is named additional
insured. Service Provider's aggregate liability is capped at the fees paid in the prior
twelve (12) months. Each party indemnifies the other for its own acts and omissions.

## Article 10 — Term & Termination

Initial term twelve (12) months, renewing automatically for successive annual terms.
Termination for cause on thirty (30) days' written notice with opportunity to cure;
termination without cause on sixty (60) days' written notice. On termination: all program
documentation prepared for Operator is delivered to Operator; outstanding invoices are due
immediately; earned milestone fees are non-refundable; resident records and consents are
handled per Article 11 and applicable law.

## Article 11 — Confidentiality & Legal Compliance

All participant information is protected consistent with applicable federal and state
confidentiality law — **including 42 C.F.R. Part 2 and HIPAA where and to the extent they
apply** — and with the parties' own consent and minimum-necessary practices. No participant
information is disclosed except as required by law or with the participant's specific
written, revocable consent. This obligation survives termination.

**Qualified-application acknowledgment.** The parties acknowledge that (a) the
characterization of resident occupancy under Iowa Code ch. 562A (including any § 562A.5
analysis) remains subject to confirmation by qualified Iowa counsel and is not resolved or
created by this Agreement; (b) neither party asserts by this Agreement that it is a
HIPAA-covered entity or a Part 2 program — applicability is determined under those laws;
(c) any notice that applicable law requires to be delivered in nonelectronic form (including
any applicable eviction, termination, default, or right-to-cure notice) is delivered and
recorded in the required form, and electronic delivery alone is never treated as legally
sufficient for such a notice; and (d) safety-response and removal procedures are exercised
consistent with applicable law as confirmed by counsel, not with characterizations assumed
in prior drafts.

## Article 12 — Dispute Resolution

Good-faith negotiation, then non-binding mediation in Polk County, Iowa, then binding AAA
arbitration in Des Moines. Iowa law governs. The prevailing party recovers reasonable
attorney's fees.
