## 1. Executive conclusion

Grace For Addictions has more substance than its present website communicates. The problem is not a lack of programs, heart, or vision. The problem is that the site contains several generations of GFA at once:

- the original founder-led life-coaching organization;
- a volunteer coaching network that may no longer reflect current people or availability;
- a developing Recovery Community Organization;
- a statewide recovery-infrastructure strategy built around RecoveryOS, the VRCC, recovery housing, reentry, navigation, and community partnerships;
- legacy clinical, privacy, neuroscience, chatbot, event, contact, governance, and program claims that are outdated, unsupported, or inconsistent with GFA's current nonclinical guardrails.

RecoveryPeople's strongest public-facing achievement is disciplined institutional clarity. It speaks about recovery as an ecosystem, identifies what it builds, proves selected claims with numbers, gives supporters distinct ways to participate, and ends with a credible future state. GFA should adopt that clarity without copying RecoveryPeople's language, funding story, program structure, or Texas-specific claims.

The recommended public position is:

> **Grace For Addictions is a peer-led Recovery Community Organization building the relational and digital connections that help recovery endure. GFA connects people and families with peer support, recovery community, practical navigation, recovery housing and reentry pathways, and compassionate support across life's transitions. GFA is nonclinical, multi-pathway, trauma-aware, dignity-centered, and free to participants.**

This wording is a proposed synthesis. "Peer-led," "across Iowa," "free to participants," and every named service must be checked against current governing evidence and actual capacity before publication.

## 3. What RecoveryPeople does especially well

The primary reference was RecoveryPeople's [Get Involved](https://recoverypeople.org/get-involved/) page, supported by its [home page](https://recoverypeople.org/), [programs](https://recoverypeople.org/programs/), [mission and values](https://recoverypeople.org/about/mission-vision-and-values/), and [history and impact](https://recoverypeople.org/about/history-and-impact/).

### 3.1 Its speaking structure

RecoveryPeople generally follows this sequence:

1. **Possibility:** Recovery is possible.
2. **Relational truth:** Recovery does not happen alone.
3. **Systems insight:** Communities need systems that support recovery.
4. **Organizational role:** RecoveryPeople builds that ecosystem.
5. **Concrete needs:** housing, trained people, sustainable organizations, protective policies.
6. **Capabilities:** housing quality, workforce development, organizational strengthening, advocacy, research, and standards.
7. **Proof:** selected quantities attached to those capabilities.
8. **Funding mechanism:** a plain explanation of why private support matters.
9. **Segmented invitations:** give, sponsor a documentary, or become a mission partner.
10. **Future state:** recovery is expected, not rare.

This is strong because every paragraph performs one job. The language is short, declarative, and easy to remember. Inspiration is tied to mechanism; mechanism is tied to evidence; evidence is tied to action.

### 3.2 Voice characteristics worth adopting

| RecoveryPeople strength | Why it works | GFA adaptation | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Recovery is an ecosystem." | Converts a complex theory into a memorable idea. | Explain GFA as the connective tissue among people, peers, housing, reentry, community, and digital access. | Do not imply ownership or control of independent partners. |
| Short, controlled repetition | Builds momentum without sounding academic. | Use "Connection prevents crisis" as the organizing refrain. | Do not repeat slogans so often that participant needs disappear. |
| Peers are presented as builders and professionals | Moves lived experience from testimonial to authority. | Show how lived experience shapes governance, design, delivery, and accountability. | Publish governance percentages or roles only after verification. |
| Programs are grouped by capabilities | Prevents a list of disconnected activities. | Group GFA's work into Connect, Navigate, Belong, Stabilize, and Build—or another approved architecture. | Do not invent active programs to fill categories. |
| Quantified proof appears next to the claim it supports | Makes impact inspectable. | Attach verified GFA measures to the relevant pathway. | Do not combine overlapping participants, referrals, contacts, or appointments. |
| Supporters receive distinct roles | Makes "get involved" actionable. | Separate Give, Volunteer, Partner, Refer, and Advocate. | Each pathway must have a real owner and response process. |
| Donor copy explains the operating gap | Answers why donations are needed. | Explain what free-to-participant access requires: people, connection, transportation, housing support, technology, safety, and continuity—as verified. | Do not borrow RecoveryPeople's reimbursement explanation unless it is true for GFA. |
| Ends with a future condition | Converts donation into system change. | End with "I matter. I belong. My future is not finished." or another approved GFA future state. | Keep the participant, not the organization, at the center. |

### 3.3 What should not be copied

GFA should borrow its narrative discipline—not its exact wording, site structure, metrics, funding mechanics, or defects.

## 5. Locked ARC/BARC-10 language

This section is the governing language for future GFA materials unless formally superseded.

Recovery capital refers to the personal, relational, material, and community resources a person can draw upon to begin and sustain recovery from substance-use-related challenges. It shifts attention from deficits and symptoms alone toward strengths, relationships, stability, opportunity, and belonging.

Recovery capital is commonly understood across three broad levels:

- **Personal recovery capital:** physical and emotional well-being, coping capacity, confidence, knowledge, life skills, and sense of purpose.
- **Social recovery capital:** supportive relationships, family connections, peers, recovery-supportive networks, and belonging.
- **Community recovery capital:** safe housing, meaningful work or education, financial and transportation resources, accessible services, and welcoming community environments.

The original ARC contains 50 statements across 10 domains. The BARC-10 contains 10 statements and produces one overall recovery-capital score. The BARC-10 does not produce reliable separate domain scores.

For GFA, the BARC-10 is:

- a participant-guided conversation and progress-reflection tool;
- a way to recognize strengths and identify support a participant may want;
- a potential aggregate program-outcome measure when consent, privacy, and data-quality rules are satisfied.

It is not:

- a diagnostic instrument;
- a treatment recommendation;
- a risk or dangerousness score;
- an eligibility or housing-admission gate;
- a compliance, punishment, discharge, or surveillance tool;
- proof that recovery will or will not succeed.

Use "initiate and sustain recovery," not "maintain sobriety," as the general GFA framing. Higher recovery capital is associated with positive outcomes in research; it does not guarantee treatment completion, abstinence, or long-term recovery.

Primary references: [ARC research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1465-3362.2012.00489.x), [BARC-10 validation](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/15835/1/Best%20-Development%20and%20validation%20of%20a%20Brief%20Assessment%20of%20Recovery%20Capital%20(BARC-10)%20(AM).pdf), and the [2024 systematic review](https://www.globalwn.org/files/2025-07/Bunaciu,%20et;%20al.,%20Measuring%20Recovery%20Capital%20for%20people%20recovering.pdf).

### Privacy/legal basis for review

HHS explains that HIPAA obligations attach to covered entities and business associates; health information becomes PHI in the HIPAA sense when held or transmitted by those regulated actors. See [HHS Covered Entities and Business Associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html) and [HHS cloud guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html). Non-HIPAA health technologies may still face FTC requirements, including the Health Breach Notification Rule. See [FTC health-data guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach).

IRS rules require certain exemption applications and annual filings to be available for public inspection/copying; that is not the same as a requirement to publish an unredacted bylaws web page. See the [IRS disclosure overview](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organization-public-disclosure-and-availability-requirements). Legal counsel should determine exactly which approved documents and redactions GFA should make available and how.

### 7.2 Audience design

Every public page should identify its primary audience. GFA currently speaks to participants, families, donors, referral partners, churches, volunteers, policymakers, and the general public in the same sections.

Use distinct pathways:

| Audience | Primary question | Primary action |
|---|---|---|
| Person seeking support | "Can someone meet me where I am?" | Request connection / explore support |
| Family or loved one | "How can I support someone without controlling them?" | Family/loved-one support pathway |
| Referral partner | "What does GFA provide, and how do I make a warm referral?" | Refer with participant knowledge/consent |
| Donor | "What does my support make possible?" | Give monthly, one time, or to an approved campaign |
| Volunteer | "Where can I contribute safely and effectively?" | Apply to a defined role |
| Community partner | "What can we build together?" | Partnership inquiry |
| Public-system leader/funder | "What gap does GFA close?" | Review model, evidence, infrastructure, and partnership options |

### 7.3 Global copy and presentation

Across the site:

- Replace "addict," "criminal," "those with criminal records," "rock bottom," "relapse," and "maintain sobriety" with approved person-first, multi-pathway language appropriate to context.
- Prefer "people impacted by the justice system" or "people navigating reentry."
- Prefer "return to use" when clinically and contextually appropriate.
- Use "participant," "person receiving support," "resident," "peer," "Recovery Coach," and "Navigator" precisely.
- Replace "trauma-informed care" with "trauma-aware practice" unless the referenced service is actually clinical care delivered by an authorized provider.
- Do not call peer services treatment, therapy, clinical care, case management, medical care, or crisis response.
- Do not say "secure," "encrypted," "anonymous," "confidential," "HIPAA-compliant," "24/7," "immediate," or "always available" without documented, current support.
- Do not present faith as a default pathway. Explain that faith-grounded support is available by participant choice and services are open without religious requirement.
- Use sentence case rather than excessive all-caps.
- Remove typographical errors, empty quotation marks, duplicate words, inconsistent capitalization, raw field names, and obsolete dates.

### 7.4 Global forms and safety

Replace the same generic contact form on every page with purpose-specific paths:

- Get support
- Refer someone
- Recovery-housing inquiry/application
- Volunteer
- Partner
- General contact

Each form should:

- ask only what is needed for the stated purpose;
- explain why each sensitive field is requested;
- distinguish optional from required fields;
- state who will see the submission;
- link to the applicable privacy notice and consent choices;
- disclose ordinary email/SMS risks where relevant;
- give a verified response window and service hours;
- state that the form is not continuously monitored and is not a crisis service;
- provide 911/988 information without implying that every difficult moment is a crisis;
- route to a named human-owned queue;
- preserve separate Grace House and EJWRH application paths, organizations, eligibility criteria, disclosures, and decision ownership.
