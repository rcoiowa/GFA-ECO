    01 · The journey and the account boundary
    1. Front door → Lobby

      A real screen transition, not an anchor that scrolls down the exterior page. Keep direct access to support and an accessible menu.

    2. Lobby → Front desk or kiosk

      Human help and self-directed exploration have equal standing. “I’m not sure” gets a useful route.

    3. Kiosk → A concrete next step

      Browse openly, assemble a temporary plan, print it, or ask for a callback with contact permission and no mandatory account.

    4. Account → Continuity

      Ask at the moment someone wants saving, reminders, private messaging, personal progress, or ongoing support. Keep voluntary content and data-sharing choices separate.

    Without an accountWith account + relevant consentAuthorized staff only
    Public meeting listings and expectations; support contacts; resource information; housing requirements; learning samples; temporary plan and printing; callback request concept.Saved plan; personal check-ins; private messages; ongoing coaching requests; reminders; application status; optional daily tracks.Circle occurrence logs; protected attendance; assigned participant follow-up; scoped reviews and corrections; aggregate reporting.

    Never gate: emergency information, basic contact details, “what happens next,” privacy explanations, or general resource discovery. Housing applications should follow the separate approved apply → receipt → optional account flow.

    03 · What would make this substantially better?

    Launch essentials

    1. A real human handoff. Named queue owner, safe contact method, staffed response window, acknowledgment, and closure.
    2. A trustworthy community board. Owner, verified date, accessibility, location, format, and cancellation state for every listing.
    3. Guest continuity. Temporary plan, print option, and a clear route to save later without duplicate intake.
    4. One app session home. Public .center links into .app when private continuity starts; test return paths and authentication.
    5. Shared-device privacy. Session warning and reset, no persistent guest data, no displayed staff records; staff use a separate authenticated workspace.
    6. Equivalent access. Keyboard navigation, plain-language view, reduced motion, reviewed translations, and low-bandwidth fallback.

    Next-stage enhancements

    1. Grace AI navigation. Opt-in, clearly identified as AI, resource source/date shown, easy human handoff, no implied live person.
    2. Optional daily tracks. Recovery, practical skills, reflection, and faith-based learning, with explicit choices and an easy off switch.
    3. Location-aware resources. County or ZIP chosen by the person; no location permission required to browse.
    4. Personalized first steps. Match expressed goals and access needs, without risk scoring or inferring emotions.
    5. Partner corrections. A way to report stale information and assign review to a responsible owner.
    6. Feedback without surveillance. “Did you find a useful next step?” Separate page visits, inquiries, delivered services, attendance and outcomes.
    04 · Proposed circle log and authority model

    Suggested home: Coach Workspace → Recovery Circles, plus the same scoped function in designated facilitator/program workspaces. Public kiosks show discovery, not staff record entry.

    ActionProposed authorization
    Create or complete an occurrenceAssigned coach or designated facilitator for that program/site.
    Add or amend named attendanceExplicit attendance permission scoped to the occurrence; participant visibility and privacy rules remain enforced.
    Review, correct, or voidScoped program manager or delegated reviewer; correction reason and history retained.
    See cross-program reportingAuthorized leadership/impact role; aggregate by default, drill-down only with separate permission.

    Recommended record: program and series; occurrence ID; partner site; delivery format; start/end with timezone; co-facilitators; status (draft, scheduled, completed, cancelled, corrected/voided); headcount and counting basis; optional lawful named attendance; topic; ICARE/domain/theme; source; recorder; review state; duplicate key; audit trail. Confirm the taxonomy before implementation.

    Reporting rule: one completed occurrence = one meeting. Named attendance and anonymous headcount stay separate. Count visits distinctly from unique people. Link to service-event attribution only through an approved, idempotent write path; do not synthesize individual events from a group headcount. Keep residence compliance evidence separate from community meeting outcomes.

    Required next checks: approved action-level access, database constraints and grants, duplicate prevention, corrections, tenant/program/site isolation, mixed guest/member attendance, counts that include facilitators, and an end-to-end synthetic test. No new roles or permission grants are adopted by this prototype.
